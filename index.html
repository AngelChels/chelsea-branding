Belonging was never the question. Opportunity is.

I grew up in Douala, Cameroon, curious about everything and happiest when I was figuring out how something worked. Problem solving was never a skill I had to learn. It was simply who I was. So when I stepped into tech, I thought the hard part was done.

It wasn’t. In my studies and later in my career, I looked around and rarely saw anyone who looked like me. Quietly, that chipped away at my confidence. I wondered whether I really belonged in the room, even while I was doing the work.

The doubt was never about my ability.

It came from not seeing people like me in the room. So now I do something about it. I show up, I speak, I share what I’m learning, and I back the people coming up behind me, so that nobody has to feel that alone. Talent was never the missing piece. Access, representation and being backed were.

I want to be proof that you can defy the stereotype, dream big, and take that first step. And I want to make that first step feel a little less lonely for the next woman who walks in.

Three principles I work by

Technical credibility

Do the work, and do it well.

I design and run CI/CD pipelines at one of the UK’s largest building societies. My automation has cut deployment failures by around 30% and saved teams an estimated 15 or more hours per sprint.

Finalist, Women in Tech Excellence Awards

Visibility

Your work deserves to be seen.

Good work does not always speak for itself, so I speak up for it, and I help other people do the same. I took the stage at Empower Together to talk about getting seen with confidence, and I’m now a finalist at the Women in Tech Excellence Awards. When people can see your work, doors open.

Making space

Success means more when it opens the door for someone else.

This is the part that lights me up. I mentor junior engineers at work, I’m part of our engineering communities of practice, and I’m lining up more mentoring and volunteering, because I know what it feels like to wonder if you belong. Nobody should have to guess whether there is a seat for them. I want to help you pull one up.
